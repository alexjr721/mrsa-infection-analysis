PhysioNet Hospital-Onset Infection Cohort · MRSA

Colonization pressure vs. antibiotic exposure

Two matched case-control cohorts, drawn from the same Mass General Brigham dataset, isolate different candidate drivers of hospital-onset MRSA: a ward's colonization pressure (environmental analysis) versus a patient's own antibiotic exposure history (patient analysis). This dashboard compares what each actually predicts.

Cohort summary

Sample sizes and demographics for each matched cohort, case vs. control.

Age is balanced between case and control in the environmental cohort (matched on age ±5y) but diverges in the patient cohort — control mean age (66.3) runs about 4 years above case mean age (62.3), consistent with that cohort not being matched on demographics.

Methodology

Matched retrospective case-control design across Mass General Brigham inpatient admissions, May 2015–July 2024. The environmental cohort matches controls to cases on age, sex, prior surgery, room length-of-stay, and antibiotic exposure history — isolating the effect of ward-level colonization pressure. The patient cohort instead matches on hospital ward, admission month/year, and room length-of-stay — isolating the effect of a patient's own antibiotic exposure, at the cost of leaving age/sex unmatched (hence the explicit adjustment in that model).

Caveats

  • The two cohorts differ in matching design and composition, so the AUC comparison above is directional, not strictly apples-to-apples.
  • In the patient model, age and sex are the dominant, most significant terms — most individual antibiotic-class coefficients are not statistically significant on their own.
  • Pseudo-R² is small in both models (environmental 0.007, patient 0.030 adjusted) — these predictors explain only a modest share of who acquires MRSA.
